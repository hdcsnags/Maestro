### Batch Pattern Analysis

Across Batch A1, exactly half the entries (9 of 18) are **COHERENT**, proving that the pipeline can distill rigorous Hebrew and Greek into piercing pastoral fruit. When grammar serves the text (e.g., *vayman* in Jonah 1:17, *harpu* in Ps 46:10, *me’arat paritsim* in Jer 7:11, and the transitive verb chain in Isa 58:6), it clarifies the verse's force for devotion without academic pretense.

Where **DRIFT** recurs, it stems from three habits:
1. **Scope Expansion:** Cards importing subsequent verses to resolve tension prematurely (e.g., citing Ps 22:24, 27 in Ps 22:1; importing Rom 8:29 into Rom 8:28) instead of resting in the assigned text.
2. **Doctrinal Overreach:** Reinterpreting relational posture as dogmatic orthodoxy (Ps 145:18 turning *be’emet* into doctrinal accuracy rather than unvarnished prayer).
3. **Displaced Practice:** Cross-pollination between paired verses (Entry 1 consumed the application intended for Matthew 27:46, forcing Entry 2 to improvise a new takeaway).

**Grammar: Fruit vs. Filler**
Grammar is fruit when lexical reality unlocks physical imagery or Hebrew narrative logic. It becomes **FILLER** when indulging in pedantic syntax trivia that helps no one at 6am (Heb 10:31's confused "compound verb") or hyper-hedging that ends up **CONTRADICTING** the insight itself (Gen 16:13 claiming *El Roi* is difficult Hebrew). In Phil 4:13, the **INSIGHT_SUSPECT** label is warranted: the card rightly caught what the insight fumbled regarding textual transmission.
