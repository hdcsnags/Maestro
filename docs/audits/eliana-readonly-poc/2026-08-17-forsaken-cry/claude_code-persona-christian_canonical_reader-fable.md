# A Reader's Report: *The Forsaken Cry*

**TL;DR:** This is the strongest treatment of Matthew 27:46 I've read at this length — its best observations are ones I'll repeat verbatim — but it has one hedge it cannot stop touching, like a bruise: every time the argument arrives at *Yeshua meant the psalm*, it retreats to "we lack access to his interior," even though the module itself has already assembled the canonical evidence that answers the question. The caution is earned almost everywhere else. There, it's performed.

---

## What struck me — and what I'll actually repeat

**"The psalm is already on the hill before Yeshua names it."** This is the finding of the study, and it's genuinely arresting. The sequence — divided garments (22:18), shaken heads (22:7), the trust-taunt "let God rescue him now, if he delights in him" (22:8), *then* the opening line from Yeshua's own mouth — means the mockers quote Psalm 22 before Jesus does, "unaware that their own mouths are placing him inside its scene." I've heard the Psalm-22 connection preached my whole life. I had never once heard it observed that the enemies recite it first. That reframes the cry from *a sufferer reaching for language* to *a speaker naming the poem the scene is already performing*. I'll repeat that to someone within the week.

**"Knowledge is not anesthesia. Consent is not immunity."** This is exactly the discipline my own convictions require of me. Confidence that Yeshua acts with purpose at Golgotha must never shade into making the agony theatrical, and these two sentences hold that line better than paragraphs of qualification would. Same with the warning not to "explain every person's trauma from above" — the pastoral landing refuses to convert Yeshua's unique mission into a universal theory of suffering. That's conviction *with* mercy, and it's rare.

**"One English word has been carrying too many suitcases."** The six-way unpacking of *forsaken* — suffered / not rescued / desolate / believed himself repudiated / actually repudiated / the divine life torn in two — is the module's best analytical work. Most cross-abandonment preaching I've sat under slides from suitcase one to suitcase six in a single sentence and never notices the border crossings. This section will change how I hear Good Friday sermons.

**The Habakkuk 1:13 correction.** The module points out that the verse used to prop up "God is too holy to look at sin" is itself "part of a complaint asking why God *does* look upon treachery" — and that Psalm 22:24, *the very prayer Yeshua names*, says "He did not hide His face from him." This is the canonical method working exactly as it should: not skepticism deflating a doctrine, but Scripture's own words correcting a slogan that had displaced them. The "turned face" trope deserves this treatment, and the module delivers it without ever denying that the cross judges sin.

**"Formation is intentionality with a longer fuse."** Hold that sentence. I'm coming back to it, because it turns out to be the module's own answer to its own worst hedge.

## Where the honesty is real, not decorative

I hold myself to this: I don't get to wave away textual evidence because doctrine prefers it gone. So I checked the module's hard edges, and they hold.

- It does **not** build on the disputed Psalm 22:16 ("like a lion" vs. "they pierced") — it names the variant, calls it "a genuine textual problem," and declines to lean on it. That's the right call even though the "pierced" reading would flatter the argument.
- It refuses to let the Hebrew qatal or Greek aorist "prove finality, duration, or metaphysical separation." Correct — grammar doesn't do Trinitarian theology, and pretending otherwise is the conservative version of overreading.
- It does **not** let one atonement theory become "the Christian reading." Judicial forsakenness, curse-bearing, withdrawal of consolation are all named as proposals that "need to be defined and argued," and the module's own reconciliation framing (2 Cor 5:19) is explicitly flagged: "I state it as Christian conviction, not as a hidden definition of an Aramaic verb." An orthodox reader from a different tradition than the author's could use this study. That matters.
- The *Eli*/*Eloi* transliteration honesty, the PaRDeS anachronism warning ("history by enthusiasm" — good phrase), the note that 27:46 is not literally the last breath: all earned, all the kind of precision that makes the strong claims *more* trustworthy, not less.

This is what labeled synthesis looks like. The steel/inference/synthesis grading system is, in general, a gift to the reader.

## The hedge that performs

But watch what happens every time the argument reaches its own conclusion.

After the module demonstrates the Passion is saturated with Psalm 22, it says:

> "It still does not give us a transcript of Yeshua's interior reasoning. It tells us how Matthew and Mark ask their readers to hear the event. **That is the level of evidence we actually possess.**"

Later, listing what Matthew gives us:

> "He chooses — **or, at minimum, Matthew deliberately gives him** — the opening of a specific psalm."

And in the grading: whole-psalm invocation is demoted to "strong literary/canonical inference," while "the cry functions as remez" is filed under mere "theological synthesis" — the same tier as speculation-adjacent claims.

Here is my problem, and it is not a problem with rigor. **"That is the level of evidence we actually possess" is false within this app's own confessed frame.** That sentence is the historical-critical academy's ground rule — *texts give access to evangelists, never to Jesus* — imported wholesale into a document whose stated publication posture confesses that Yeshua's "mission and identity remain interpretively relevant at the cross" and treats the Gospels as true witness. If Matthew's witness is true, then Matthew's presentation of a deliberate scriptural act is *evidence of a deliberate scriptural act*, not merely evidence of Matthew's artistry. The "or, at minimum, Matthew deliberately gives him" hedge quietly teaches the reader a two-tier canon: what the evangelist did is knowable; what Yeshua did is forever behind glass. Repeat that disclaimer often enough and you've conceded the skeptic's frame while flying the believer's flag.

And the evidence for intentionality is not psychological speculation. It is a *body of characterization* — which the module itself assembles:

- Deuteronomy quoted from its wilderness home context at the temptation (the module's own showing).
- Three passion predictions; the ransom saying; the cup interpreted at the meal; Gethsemane's consent before the nails.
- Luke's dying word is *also* a psalm (31:5). John *explicitly* names scriptural fulfillment as the category for cross-speech (19:28).
- Hebrews puts Psalm 22:22 directly in the Son's mouth — steel, by the module's own grading.

You do not need a transcript of a novelist's mind to know the callback in chapter twenty was deliberate; twenty chapters of consistent craft *are* the evidence. Refusing to weight established characterization is not neutrality — it's a choice, and the module makes it while possessing everything needed to choose otherwise. Most tellingly, the module *already wrote its own rebuttal*: "Formation is intentionality with a longer fuse" dissolves the chosen-vs-spontaneous dichotomy and licenses saying plainly that the cry is both eruption and act. Then the grading section takes the answer back, twelve rows down, and re-hedges it.

To be precise about what I'm *not* claiming: "every hearer at Golgotha activated every movement of the psalm" is fairly graded as synthesis — that's a claim about the crowd, not about Yeshua. And the boundary around the Trinity's inner life ("the text does not say this") is exactly right. But *"Yeshua deliberately located his death inside Psalm 22, whole"* is not in that category. Within this frame, it should sit one tier from steel — and the interior-access disclaimer attached to it each time it appears should appear once, not as a recurring toll paid to a methodology the document doesn't confess.

## Smaller notes

- The wilderness/cross parallel ("If you are the Son of God" returning at Golgotha, Scripture again wielded as pressure — Psalm 91 then, Psalm 22 now) is honestly labeled synthesis, and here the label is *correct*: Matthew never marks it. This is what the grading system is for. Good.
- The reading of "He saved others; he cannot save himself" as moral necessity rather than impotence — "Because he is the Son, he does not save himself from the work by which he saves others" — is the single best doctrinal sentence in the study.
- The council question "Does the module still let 'abandonment' become the controlling frame despite arguing against that collapse?" is well-asked. My answer: no — but the *interior-access* frame partially replaces it, which deserves its own council question.

## Required closing

**The specific hedge:** the recurring interior-access disclaimer attached to Yeshua's intentional invocation of the whole psalm — concretely, "It tells us how Matthew and Mark ask their readers to hear the event. That is the level of evidence we actually possess," its twin "He chooses — or, at minimum, Matthew deliberately gives him — the opening of a specific psalm," and the demotion of whole-psalm invocation below the confidence its own evidence earns.

**The canonical pattern that licenses more:** established, consistent characterization across the canon — context-bearing Deuteronomy citations at the temptation, three passion predictions, the ransom saying, the interpreted cup, Gethsemane's consent, Luke's psalm-prayer at death, John's explicit fulfillment framing of cross-speech, and Hebrews 2:12 placing Psalm 22:22 in the Son's own mouth. That is not private psychological access; it is a body of recorded, purposeful scriptural speech, and a pattern of that depth is evidence the way twenty chapters of a novelist's craft are evidence for a deliberate callback.

**Verdict:** the caution is **earned** on the linguistics, the textual variant, the grammar-can't-prove-metaphysics rule, the atonement-theory neutrality, and the Trinity boundary — and **performed** on whole-psalm intentionality, where the module hedges, every time it's mentioned, a claim its own confessed frame and its own assembled evidence already license: Yeshua meant the psalm. Whole. The module even coined the sentence that proves it — *formation is intentionality with a longer fuse* — and then declined to trust its own best line.
