Those requests conflict with the collection’s best pastoral instinct: truthful prayer does not require a successful emotional outcome. Some protective additions are welcome but introduce inaccurate distinctions, especially day 53’s separation of “trials” from harm inflicted by someone else.

**Overall — educational: 4/5.** The collection teaches substantially more than devotional slogans. Repeated assurances that Greek or Hebrew is stronger, more physical, or more decisive than English can train readers to distrust competent translations unnecessarily. I would adjust one priority in the brief: the educational test should be whether readers understand the verse better, not whether every card supplies something they could never have guessed.

**The 72-day cycle**

Seventy-two is a workable launch collection, but I would not treat an unchanged 72-card loop as the finished educational experience for a daily user’s year. A 365-day user encounters five complete cycles plus five cards: most cards appear five times, some six. Repetition can deepen prayer and memory, but this collection already repeats several application patterns before the first cycle ends.

Keep the strongest cards as recurring anchors and preserve the linked Scripture chains. For later cycles, give the same passage a genuinely different observation or practice, or progressively expand the collection with reviewed material. Do not rush toward 365 merely to eliminate repetition. The useful product test is whether a returning reader encounters a fresh opportunity to understand and practice the passage—not whether every date has an unseen verse.

My principal disagreement with the brief is therefore about novelty and scoring: **textual fidelity and a truthful, doable response should outrank a surprising lexical lesson.** Several of the best cards need no linguistic revelation to become worth opening at 6:40 am.
