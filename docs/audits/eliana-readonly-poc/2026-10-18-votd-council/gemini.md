### 5. Best Five / Weakest Five

**Best Five (the standard the rest should reach):**
- **Day 66 — Genesis 50:20:** Pairs razor-sharp Hebrew observation (the repeated verb *chashav* holding two real, opposite intentions over the same event) with the single best trauma-aware pastoral guardrail in the corpus ("Joseph could only say this decades later, from safety, with the good already visible — if you can't see anything like that yet, this verse doesn't put you on a deadline to find it").
- **Day 19 — Jeremiah 29:11:** Rescues a famously misquoted verse without a trace of pedantry by grounding it in the false-prophet warning three verses earlier (29:8) and landing the bridge effortlessly ("The exiles' timetable was theirs; the heart behind the promise is his, and it hasn't changed").
- **Day 50 — 1 Corinthians 13:4:** Connects *physioutai* ("puffed up") to Paul's running correction of Corinthian pride (8:1), highlights that all five qualities in Greek are active verbs, and protects vulnerable readers with a clear boundary ("a coworker, a child, a friend, not someone who harms you... Patience doesn't mean staying in reach of harm").
- **Day 16 — Philippians 4:13:** Explains both the textual variant ("the one who keeps strengthening me" vs. "Christ") and the poverty-and-plenty context of verses 11–12 in two crisp sentences, reframing the verse from ambition-fuel into endurance.
